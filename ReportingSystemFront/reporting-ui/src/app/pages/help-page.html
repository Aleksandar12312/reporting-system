Help

Everything you need to find your way through the Reporting System: what it shows, what the colours and numbers mean, how to mark a part as OK, and what to do when a message appears.

What the Reporting System does

The Reporting System watches bill-recycler machines (units) and shows which ones need attention. It compares every unit with other units of the same model, so a unit is only flagged when it does clearly worse than machines like it.

It answers two questions, in this order:

  1. Is something wrong with a part? The scanner (reads the notes) or the recycler (stores and pays out notes).
  2. How does the unit do overall? The tier: how often it fails compared with its model.

The numbers come from the backend and are recalculated about every 5 minutes. The time of the last calculation is shown at the top right ("Updated hh:mm"). The window for the statistics is the last {{ meta.windowDays() }} days.

Where to start

  1. Open Part problems (the start page). Choose Scanner or Recycler; the worst units are at the top.
  2. Click a unit to open it. Read the message at the top: what is wrong and how it compares with similar units.
  3. After the part was cleaned, checked or repaired, press Mark scanner as OK / Mark recycler as OK.
  4. For an overall view, use Unit ranking: part problems first, then the tier.

To jump to a known unit, type its ID in the Unit ID box at the top and press Open unit.

Scanner and recycler problems

For every unit the backend checks both parts and gives each one a status:

StatusShown asMeaning
Active orange box on the unit page Something is wrong with the part now.
Recurring red box, "…again, N days after it was marked OK" The problem came back within 14 days after someone marked the part as OK – the last fix did not last.
OK no box Nothing to show for this part.

The scanner

Measured as the share of rejected notes (notes the scanner could not accept, out of all notes it read). Example: "Rejects 30% of notes (similar units: 8%)."

A scanner with many rejects may need cleaning.

The recycler

Measured as recycler faults per day. Example: "0.67 faults per day (similar units: 0.15)."

A recycler with many faults may need to be checked.

The suggestions are hints, not instructions: the system only sees the numbers. The backend decides the status; the page never sets its own limits.

Marking a part as OK

  1. On the unit page press Mark scanner as OK, Mark recycler as OK, or Mark all as OK (shown when both parts have a problem).
  2. Enter your name (required, up to 100 characters) and, if you like, a note such as "scanner cleaned" (up to 500 characters). Your name is remembered for next time.
  3. Press Mark as OK. The box disappears at once and a short confirmation appears.

What happens next:

  • The backend starts counting that part again from now. Old data no longer counts, so the message does not come back because of the past.
  • While there is too little new data, the unit page shows a grey line: "Scanner marked OK on … by …. Collecting new data since …". This is expected, not an error.
  • If the part does badly again within 14 days, it becomes Recurring.
  • Every "marked OK" is kept in OK history at the bottom of the unit page (time, part, name, note).

Pressing it twice is harmless, but it restarts the counting again.

Tiers

The tier is the overall picture of the unit and is shown after the part problems.

TierMeaning
Fails at least 4 times as often as a typical unit of its model.
Fails at least 2 times as often.
Normal, or too few faults (fewer than 3) to judge.
Cannot be compared yet: there is no typical value for its model.

Scope (only for WATCH and CRITICAL units with at least 8 faults): PART – one component causes at least half of the faults (see Top component); GENERAL – the faults are spread over the whole machine.

Numbers and terms

Similar units / Model median
The value of a typical unit of the same model (the middle value). "—" means it is not known yet.
Ratio
How many times worse than a typical unit of the same model. 3.0× = three times as many faults.
Faults / day
Faults divided by the days the unit was observed.
Days / Days observed
How many days of the window the unit was watched (shorter for new units).
Notes read / Faults counted
How much data a part decision is based on, since Counting since.
Counting since
Start of the data used for a part: the start of the window, or the day after it was last marked OK.
Top component
The component with the most faults, with its count and share, e.g. RC (30, 73%).
Component, Source, Type (events)
Where on the machine an event happened, what the event was (e.g. JAMMED), and its category.
Times
All times are shown in your local time.

Pages

Part problems
Units with a scanner or recycler problem, worst first (recurring, then active, then the highest ratio). Show all units also lists units without a problem. Filter by serial number, ID or model.
Unit ranking
All units with part problems first, then by tier. Cards at the top filter the list. Tabs: Problematic (WATCH and CRITICAL), Worst (the N worst), All units. Click a column header to sort (click again to reverse, a third time to go back to the default order).
Unit page
Part messages and Mark as OK, then tier and statistics, faults by component and kind (click a row to see those fault events), the newest events with search and filters, and the OK history. Filters work on the loaded events only – raise "Newest events to load" (up to 1000) to look further back.
By fault kind
The units with the most faults of one kind in the last {{ meta.windowDays() }} days.
Models
Per model: number of units, faults, and typical (median) and average faults per day. Click a model to see its units.
Developer mode (top right)
Shows the backend address behind each list. Useful when reporting a problem; not needed for daily work.

Every list keeps its filters in the address bar, so you can bookmark a view or send the link to a colleague.

Empty lists and missing parts

A grey note means "nothing to show", not an error:

"No unit has a scanner problem right now."
Good news – no unit is flagged for this part. Use Show all units to see all of them.
"This unit is not listed."
The backend has no statistics for this unit ID (unknown or too new).
"No faults in the last {{ meta.windowDays() }} days." / "No events for this unit."
The unit had no such events.
"No units match the current filters." / "No events match the current filters."
The filters hide everything. Press Clear filters.
"Never marked OK."
Nobody has marked a part of this unit as OK yet.
No cards at the top and no "Updated" time
The backend does not offer the summary yet. The lists still work.

Troubleshooting

Every page says the backend is not reachable
Start the backend first (port 8080), then the frontend with start-frontend.bat (or npm start), and open http://localhost:4200.
The numbers look old
They are recalculated about every 5 minutes. Press Refresh after that; check "Updated" at the top.
I marked a part as OK but nothing seems to happen later
Right after marking, the system collects new data first. A new message appears only if the part does badly again.
A unit I expect is missing from Part problems
Only units with a current problem are listed. Turn on Show all units, or check the filters.
Reporting a problem
Turn on Developer mode, take a screenshot that shows the address and the message, and note the time.
